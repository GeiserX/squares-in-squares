## 2. The jam line

A chain of w squares crosses a corridor at phi off the perpendicular. Each end of the chain is a flat face. One face touches a tooth of one staircase, and the other face touches a tooth of the other staircase. The two teeth are a whole number j of lattice steps apart across the corridor. The chain fits when the two teeth are at least w apart along the chain. That gives

`s + 2f = (√2·w + j·sin phi) / cos phi`

j has the parity of s. j = 0 at phi = 0 is the rigid 45° block. j = −1 is the line that falls as the chain turns, and j = +1 is the line that rises. The lowest point of the line j = −1 is at `sin phi = 1/(√2·w)`, where

`f* = (√(2w² − 1) − s) / 2`

The rule against held layouts (tilts from the [band](band.md) doc, to two decimals):

## 5. Turned blocks: the tartan LP and the outline search

A block cannot keep straight chains: its two long faces would stand at phi to their staircases. The generator of section 4 holds 252 where the held 261 holds 261. The held 261 shows what a block does instead. We measured it:

- Every row is rigid along u, and every column is rigid along v.
- Row j slides by `S_j` along u, and column i slips by `R_i` along v. So square (i, j) has its centre at `O + (i + S_j)·u + (j + R_i)·v`.
- S grows with j, and R falls with i (steps of 0 or 0.077 = √2·tan phi). That order keeps the squares apart.
- The outline has one jog: two rows sit one column over, and the next row is one square longer. The block is 8 × 13 plus 1.

`k123_tartan.py` uses this. For a fixed phi every centre is linear in (O, S, R), and every upright is linear in L, because it keeps its walls. With one separating axis for each pair, the smallest L is an LP of about 40 unknowns. The axes are chosen again after each step.

`k123_outline.py` searches the outline. For each site set it places the turned block, shrunk by 1 to 2 %, on a grid of 121 positions. It takes the uprights from the exact refill on the four wall lattices only, and it runs the LP on each hole that holds n squares.
